# German Assessment Architecture

Assessments check whether learners can use German for the communicative purpose of the current sublevel. They do not reward isolated grammar knowledge unless it supports a real task.

## Assessment Cadence

| Assessment type | Frequency | Purpose | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly check | Weekly or after a comparable block of scheduled activity | Confirm retrieval, recent skill development, and readiness for next modules. | Vocabulary/grammar-in-context, receptive item, production item, short reflection. |
| Module assessment | At the end of each module | Verify that the module real-world task can be completed. | U05 task evidence, module knowledge check, pronunciation or interaction check when relevant. |
| Sublevel final integrated assessment | End of each CEFR sublevel | Decide progression to next sublevel or remediation plan. | Listening, reading, spoken interaction, written production, mediation, pragmatic-cultural judgment. |

## Weekly Check Blueprint

| Component | Duration target | Scoring focus |
|---|---:|---|
| Retrieval warm-up | 3-5 min | Previously learned vocabulary, chunks, grammar, and pronunciation cues. |
| Grammar/vocabulary in context | 5-8 min | Correct form only in communicative context. |
| Listening or reading detail | 5-10 min | Main idea, key detail, next action, and inference appropriate to level. |
| Speaking or writing production | 8-15 min | Task completion, intelligibility, accuracy, register, and repair. |
| Reflection/remediation flag | 2-5 min | Confidence, blockers, and recovery recommendations. |

## Module Assessment Blueprint

| Component | Required at all levels | Scaling by level |
|---|---|---|
| Real-world task | Complete the module U05 evidence task. | A1-A2 use constrained prompts; B1-B2 add choice and justification; C1-C2 require audience and register decisions. |
| Knowledge check | Verify key grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, and pragmatic concept. | Higher levels assess source use, stance, precision, and genre control. |
| Receptive check | Short listening or reading tied to the module domain. | Input length and density follow the listening/reading progression matrices. |
| Production check | Spoken or written output depending on module objective. | Rubrics expand from task completion to nuance, cohesion, and style. |
| Remediation decision | Identify review units and concepts if needed. | Recovery plans may target grammar, vocabulary, skill, or pragmatic-cultural gaps. |

## Final Integrated Assessment by Sublevel

| Sublevel | Integrated scenario | Listening/reading evidence | Speaking/writing evidence | Mediation evidence | Progression threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1.1 | First day in a German-speaking setting | Understand profile, prices, directions, and routine details | Introduce self, buy item, write short note | Relay name, number, or direction | Complete routine tasks with heavy support. |
| A1.2 | Daily-life week with appointments and errands | Understand messages, notices, and service details | Arrange appointment, describe routine, write confirmation | Relay schedule or instruction | Complete familiar tasks with support. |
| A2.1 | Short trip with a service problem | Understand travel/service input and factual text | Narrate past event, compare options, request solution | Summarize notice or advice | Complete connected transactions independently. |
| A2.2 | Everyday independence and correspondence | Understand news, instructions, and emails | Write practical email, recommend, describe problem | Explain requirements or recommendation | Manage familiar everyday scenarios independently. |
| B1.1 | Study/work plan with disruption and opinion task | Understand accessible media and formal info | Discuss opinion, narrate, write formal request | Summarize article or form details | Sustain threshold independence. |
| B1.2 | Civic/workplace problem-solving scenario | Understand meeting/civic information | Argue with examples, write complaint/application | Mediate rules, deadlines, and options | Handle independent formal and civic tasks. |
| B2.1 | Professional briefing with data and recommendation | Understand interview/report/chart | Present data, write memo/formal email | Synthesize evidence into briefing | Produce clear detailed B2 output. |
| B2.2 | Negotiation and multi-source synthesis | Understand authentic sources and stance | Negotiate, present, write argument | Compare sources with caveats | Show B2 autonomy and register control. |
| C1.1 | Advanced policy/professional briefing | Understand complex discourse and dense source | Debate, critique, write professional briefing | Reframe specialist information | Show advanced discourse and mediation control. |
| C1.2 | High-stakes rhetoric and portfolio task | Understand dense prose and discourse framing | Negotiate diplomatically, edit and defend text | Produce register-specific transformations | Show flexible C1 fluency and style. |
| C2.1 | Expert synthesis under institutional constraint | Understand specialized/institutional input | Public defense or crisis briefing, expert response | High-fidelity synthesis with risk | Show near-native operational precision. |
| C2.2 | Final integrated mastery scenario | Understand culturally dense, expert, or ambiguous input | Oral portfolio and publishable written portfolio | Translation-aware and culturally appropriate mediation | Show mastery-level precision, nuance, and effect. |

## Rubric Dimensions

| Dimension | A1-A2 emphasis | B1-B2 emphasis | C1-C2 emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task completion | Required details and action | Complete purpose with justification | Strategic effect and audience outcome |
| Accuracy | Core forms and intelligibility | Controlled sentence and discourse grammar | Precision, nuance, and stylistic appropriacy |
| Vocabulary | Domain recall and chunks | Collocation, paraphrase, source language | Connotation, idiom, terminology, rhetorical effect |
| Receptive skill | Main point and detail | Structure, evidence, stance | Implication, ambiguity, cultural reference |
| Production | Formulae and short connected output | Coherent paragraphs and extended turns | Genre control, voice, and publishable polish |
| Interaction | Predictable turns and repair | Discussion, negotiation, facilitation | Diplomacy, expert debate, high-pressure repair |
| Mediation | Relay key details | Summarize and adapt for purpose | Transform register and preserve nuance |
| Pragmatics | Politeness and conventions | Register and institutional expectations | Cultural density, subtext, rhetorical choice |
