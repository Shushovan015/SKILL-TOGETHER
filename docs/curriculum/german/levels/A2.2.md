# German A2.2 Curriculum Architecture

This file is architecture, not learner-facing lesson copy. It defines the authoritative module and learning-unit plan for the German track. Daily sessions select activities from these learning units according to the session-length rules in `../13-session-authoring-standard.md`; one learning unit is not equivalent to one daily session.

## Sublevel Contract

- Purpose: consolidate A2 independence with narrative, preferences, problems, and correspondence.
- Entry profile: A2.1 transaction language, Perfekt foundations, connectors, everyday domains.
- Exit profile: Learner can narrate common experiences, explain preferences, write practical email, and mediate routine information.
- Skill profile: longer simple turns, familiar media, practical email, controlled narrative.
- Module count: 10.
- Learning-unit count: 50.
- Learning-unit fields: the unit ID gives CEFR sublevel and module; each row includes communicative objective, prerequisites/review, grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, listening, speaking, reading, writing, interaction, mediation, pragmatic-cultural objectives, real-world task, activity architecture with duration and priority, difficulty, assessment tags, and progression status.

## Module Sequence

| Module | Title | Communicative purpose | Grammar focus | Vocabulary focus | Pronunciation focus | Module evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M01 | Life events and short biographies | tell a simple life story and ask about milestones | Perfekt/Praeteritum of sein/haben; time connectors; preterite recognition | life stages, education, work, family events | past-tense rhythm; stress in dates | present a short biography with timeline details |
| M02 | Preferences, opinions, and reasons | express preferences and give connected reasons | weil/dass clauses; adjective comparison review; pronoun reference | opinions, tastes, entertainment, reasons, evaluation words | sentence stress for stance; connectors in speech | recommend an activity or product with reasons |
| M03 | Describing people, places, and objects | describe features and compare alternatives | adjective endings recognition; relative clauses as chunks; comparative review | appearance, personality, places, object features, dimensions | ending reduction; compound stress | describe a lost item, person, or place clearly |
| M04 | Problems, solutions, and instructions | explain a problem and understand simple instructions | imperatives; wenn clauses as noticing; modal verbs for advice | technical problems, instructions, repair, troubleshooting | imperative intonation; word groups in instructions | explain a device problem and follow repair steps |
| M05 | News, weather, and public information | understand simple news and public updates | passive recognition; time/place/manner word order; reported information chunks | weather, transport updates, local news, public notices | news-reading rhythm; vowel length in public terms | summarize a local update and say what action is needed |
| M06 | Invitations, events, and social plans | organize a social event and respond appropriately | subordinate clauses review; prepositions with events; modal verbs | events, invitations, venue, schedule, social phrases | polite intonation; reductions in RSVP phrases | plan an event and respond to an invitation |
| M07 | Experiences and recommendations | describe experiences and make practical recommendations | Perfekt consolidation; connectors deshalb/trotzdem; adverbial order | travel, restaurants, courses, services, recommendation language | contrastive stress for pros/cons; participle review | write a short review with recommendation |
| M08 | Media, hobbies, and learning strategies | talk about media use and describe learning strategies | reflexive verbs as chunks; infinitive with zu recognition; frequency structures | media, hobbies, apps, learning actions, strategies | loanword pronunciation; sentence melody in explanations | explain a learning routine and recommend a resource |
| M09 | Practical email and formality | write a practical email with suitable opening and closing | formal requests; word order after connectors; pronoun reference | email formulae, requests, attachments, deadlines, confirmations | reading formal text aloud; polite rhythm | write an email requesting information or support |
| M10 | A2.2 integrated independence project | combine A2.2 language for everyday independence and correspondence | review: Perfekt, subordinate clauses, comparison, connectors, imperatives, formal email | biography, opinions, problems, news, events, media, email | review: clause rhythm, compounds, polite intonation | complete a multi-step scenario with event planning, problem report, summary, and email |

## Sublevel Assessment Architecture

- Weekly checks: short retrieval, vocabulary/grammar-in-context, listening or reading detail, and one production item selected from current modules.
- Module assessment: integrated task evidence from U05 plus a focused knowledge check on module grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, and pragmatic-cultural conventions.
- Final integrated assessment: combines listening, reading, spoken interaction, written production, mediation, and reflection using the sublevel exit profile.
- Progression decision: learner advances when core module evidence, weekly checks, and final integrated assessment meet the thresholds in `../11-assessment-framework.md`.

## Resource Architecture

- Core resources: model dialogues or texts, vocabulary set, grammar-in-context prompt set, audio script, pronunciation cue, controlled practice bank, production rubric, and real-world task card.
- Optional resources: remedial explanations, alternate input texts, extension prompts, cultural/pragmatic notes, and assessment variants.
