# German A1.1 Curriculum Architecture

This file is architecture, not learner-facing lesson copy. It defines the authoritative module and learning-unit plan for the German track. Daily sessions select activities from these learning units according to the session-length rules in `../13-session-authoring-standard.md`; one learning unit is not equivalent to one daily session.

## Sublevel Contract

- Purpose: create first-contact survival ability and predictable classroom participation.
- Entry profile: No prior German or only isolated words.
- Exit profile: Learner can introduce self, exchange basic personal details, handle prices, directions, daily routines, and very short messages with support.
- Skill profile: short rehearsed turns, high support, slow clear input, formulaic writing.
- Module count: 10.
- Learning-unit count: 50.
- Learning-unit fields: the unit ID gives CEFR sublevel and module; each row includes communicative objective, prerequisites/review, grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, listening, speaking, reading, writing, interaction, mediation, pragmatic-cultural objectives, real-world task, activity architecture with duration and priority, difficulty, assessment tags, and progression status.

## Module Sequence

| Module | Title | Communicative purpose | Grammar focus | Vocabulary focus | Pronunciation focus | Module evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M01 | First meetings and names | start and close a first meeting, exchange names, and choose du or Sie | sein with ich/du/Sie; heissen; word order in statements and W-questions | greetings, names, country labels, polite formulae | alphabet; h in heissen; ie in Sie | hold a first-meeting dialogue with greeting, name, origin, and goodbye |
| M02 | Spelling, classroom language, and repair | spell names, ask for repetition, and use classroom instructions | imperatives as chunks; bitte; Wie bitte; buchstabieren | letters, classroom objects, learning verbs, repair phrases | German alphabet; sch/sp/st; final consonants | clarify spelling and complete a class registration card |
| M03 | Numbers, countries, and languages | give numbers, countries, languages, and simple contact details | kommen aus; sprechen; ja/nein questions; negation with nicht as chunk | 0-100, countries, languages, phone and email words | zwei/drei; ch in ich; word stress in countries | exchange phone number, country, and languages spoken |
| M04 | People and personal profiles | ask and answer basic profile questions about identity and location | regular present-tense verbs; W-questions; sentence bracket as noticing | jobs, cities, age, address, profile fields | sentence melody in questions; long and short vowels | complete and present a simple learner profile |
| M05 | Family, friends, and possession | describe close people and relationships simply | possessive articles mein/dein; haben; gender with der/die/das | family, friends, relationship nouns, basic traits | e/ae contrast; word stress in family words | introduce two people and state relationships |
| M06 | Daily routine and time | state times and describe a basic day | present tense with routine verbs; time adverb placement; um/am | clock time, weekdays, routine verbs, frequency words | u/ue contrast; rhythm in time expressions | describe today and tomorrow with three routine actions |
| M07 | Food, drinks, and preferences | order simple food and express likes, dislikes, and quantities | moechte as chunk; gern/nicht gern; accusative noticing | food, drinks, menu words, quantities | oe sound; sentence stress for preference | order a snack and answer preference questions |
| M08 | Shopping, prices, and basic requests | ask for items, prices, and quantities in a shop | haben/moechten; kein as chunk; plural noticing | shops, items, colors, prices, payment words | eu/ei contrast; numbers in prices | complete a small purchase with price confirmation |
| M09 | Around town and simple directions | name places and ask where something is | es gibt; prepositions in chunks; wo/wohin distinction as noticing | town places, directions, transport, landmarks | ich/ach ch contrast; stress in compound place names | ask for a place and follow a simple direction |
| M10 | A1.1 integrated survival project | combine A1.1 language for a first day in a German-speaking setting | review: sein, haben, regular present, W-questions, ja/nein questions, chunks with moechte | personal details, classroom, numbers, routine, food, shopping, town | review of vowel length, question melody, sch/sp/st, ch | complete a first-day scenario with profile, directions, purchase, and short message |

## Sublevel Assessment Architecture

- Weekly checks: short retrieval, vocabulary/grammar-in-context, listening or reading detail, and one production item selected from current modules.
- Module assessment: integrated task evidence from U05 plus a focused knowledge check on module grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, and pragmatic-cultural conventions.
- Final integrated assessment: combines listening, reading, spoken interaction, written production, mediation, and reflection using the sublevel exit profile.
- Progression decision: learner advances when core module evidence, weekly checks, and final integrated assessment meet the thresholds in `../11-assessment-framework.md`.

## Resource Architecture

- Core resources: model dialogues or texts, vocabulary set, grammar-in-context prompt set, audio script, pronunciation cue, controlled practice bank, production rubric, and real-world task card.
- Optional resources: remedial explanations, alternate input texts, extension prompts, cultural/pragmatic notes, and assessment variants.
