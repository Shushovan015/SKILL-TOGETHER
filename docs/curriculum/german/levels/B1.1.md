# German B1.1 Curriculum Architecture

This file is architecture, not learner-facing lesson copy. It defines the authoritative module and learning-unit plan for the German track. Daily sessions select activities from these learning units according to the session-length rules in `../13-session-authoring-standard.md`; one learning unit is not equivalent to one daily session.

## Sublevel Contract

- Purpose: build threshold independence for work, study, travel, and opinions.
- Entry profile: A2 narrative, routine transaction handling, subordinate clause recognition.
- Exit profile: Learner can explain plans, justify preferences, summarize familiar information, and handle common disruptions.
- Skill profile: multi-turn conversations, connected writing, clear standard input.
- Module count: 10.
- Learning-unit count: 50.
- Learning-unit fields: the unit ID gives CEFR sublevel and module; each row includes communicative objective, prerequisites/review, grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, listening, speaking, reading, writing, interaction, mediation, pragmatic-cultural objectives, real-world task, activity architecture with duration and priority, difficulty, assessment tags, and progression status.

## Module Sequence

| Module | Title | Communicative purpose | Grammar focus | Vocabulary focus | Pronunciation focus | Module evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M01 | Plans, goals, and future intentions | explain goals and plan next steps | werden future; infinitive with zu; purpose clauses damit/um zu as noticing | goals, milestones, planning verbs, priorities | intonation in longer explanations; schwa reduction | present a personal or learning plan with next steps |
| M02 | Education and training choices | compare training options and explain suitability | comparatives review; relative clauses; adjective endings productive intro | courses, qualifications, requirements, strengths, support | compound stress in education terms; endings | choose a course and justify suitability |

| M05 | Opinions in familiar discussions | state and support opinions in a familiar discussion | weil/obwohl; connectors; pronoun reference across sentences | society, everyday issues, advantages, disadvantages, stance phrases | contrastive stress; turn-taking intonation | participate in a short opinion discussion |
| M06 | Narrating experiences with detail | narrate experiences with sequence and evaluation | Perfekt/Praeteritum contrast; plusquamperfekt recognition; temporal clauses | events, emotions, sequence, evaluation, reflection | narrative pacing; participle stress | tell an experience and explain what changed |

| M08 | Media reports and summaries | summarize accessible media and distinguish main ideas | reported information with dass; passive recognition; connector review | news topics, media genres, evidence, summary phrases | news intonation; pausing in summaries | summarize an accessible article or audio report |

## Sublevel Assessment Architecture

- Weekly checks: short retrieval, vocabulary/grammar-in-context, listening or reading detail, and one production item selected from current modules.
- Module assessment: integrated task evidence from U05 plus a focused knowledge check on module grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, and pragmatic-cultural conventions.
- Final integrated assessment: combines listening, reading, spoken interaction, written production, mediation, and reflection using the sublevel exit profile.
- Progression decision: learner advances when core module evidence, weekly checks, and final integrated assessment meet the thresholds in `../11-assessment-framework.md`.

## Resource Architecture

- Core resources: model dialogues or texts, vocabulary set, grammar-in-context prompt set, audio script, pronunciation cue, controlled practice bank, production rubric, and real-world task card.
- Optional resources: remedial explanations, alternate input texts, extension prompts, cultural/pragmatic notes, and assessment variants.
