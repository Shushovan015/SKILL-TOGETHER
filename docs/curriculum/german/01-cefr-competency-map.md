# CEFR Competency Map

This matrix translates CEFR sublevels into concrete SkillTogether outcomes. It is an internal curriculum map, not an official certification claim.

| Level | Listening | Speaking | Reading | Writing | Interaction | Mediation | Pragmatics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1.1 | Understand slow greetings, names, numbers, countries, and classroom phrases. | Produce rehearsed introductions and simple personal facts. | Read signs, forms, short profiles, and simple messages with familiar words. | Fill forms and write short personal sentences. | Exchange names, origin, language, and basic needs with support. | Relay very simple personal or practical facts. | Use basic greetings, du/Sie awareness, bitte/danke, and polite repair phrases. |
| A1.2 | Understand short controlled dialogues about routine, home, food, shopping, time, and appointments. | Describe routine, preferences, simple needs, and plans in short connected chunks. | Read short everyday messages, menus, notices, and simple emails. | Write simple emails, requests, descriptions, and routine notes. | Participate in predictable service and classroom conversations. | Pass on simple times, prices, directions, and personal information. | Choose formal/informal address in common contexts and use basic appointment conventions. |
| A2.1 | Understand clear practical speech about travel, health, services, housing, work, and recent events. | Handle short practical conversations and describe recent experiences. | Read forms, service information, short articles, travel details, and instructions. | Write practical messages, short narratives, and problem descriptions. | Manage appointments, travel, service requests, and simple phone scripts. | Summarize simple practical information for another person. | Use polite requests, basic complaints, and context-appropriate service language. |
| A2.2 | Understand semi-authentic everyday conversations with predictable topics. | Tell simple stories, explain preferences, compare options, and give reasons. | Read longer emails, short narratives, accessible articles, and public information. | Write connected paragraphs, informal messages, and simple formal requests. | Solve common problems through clarification and simple negotiation. | Reformulate short spoken or written information in simpler language. | Use indirect questions, softer disagreement, and everyday register choices. |
| B1.1 | Understand main points in clear natural speech about work, study, plans, and opinions. | Explain experiences, plans, opinions, and reasons independently. | Read short news, blog posts, instructions, and everyday institutional texts. | Write coherent personal letters, opinions, and simple reports. | Sustain conversations through follow-up questions and reactions. | Summarize main points from short texts/audio for practical use. | Manage politeness, directness, invitations, and everyday disagreement. |
| B1.2 | Understand longer conversations, simple media, and talks with familiar structure. | Present short structured explanations and participate in B1-level discussion. | Read accessible news, workplace texts, narratives, and argument outlines. | Write formal correspondence, summaries, short reports, and supported opinions. | Maintain conversations, repair misunderstandings, and explain decisions. | Summarize and compare information from one or two manageable sources. | Use conditional politeness, appropriate email/phone conventions, and basic register shifts. |
| B2.1 | Understand authentic interviews, discussions, presentations, and media on known complex topics. | Argue a viewpoint, present structure, and discuss professional topics. | Read articles, reports, commentary, and professional documents with strategy. | Write formal emails, reports, argumentative texts, and recommendations. | Participate in discussions, defend opinions, and respond to counterpoints. | Explain key information from complex but accessible sources. | Control register in formal writing, meetings, disagreement, and recommendations. |
| B2.2 | Understand detailed authentic media, workplace speech, and extended arguments. | Debate, summarize, present, and communicate professionally with increasing fluency. | Read advanced articles, interviews, reports, and academic introductions. | Write polished reports, structured arguments, and professional correspondence. | Negotiate tasks, clarify positions, and manage extended discussions. | Summarize and adapt complex information for a non-specialist. | Use nuance, hedging, stance markers, and culturally appropriate directness. |
| C1.1 | Understand lectures, debates, professional speech, implicit stance, and dense media. | Speak fluently on complex topics with structure and precise stance. | Read academic/professional texts, commentary, and complex arguments. | Write synthesis, analysis, professional documents, and academic-style argument. | Negotiate, challenge, reformulate, and manage complex group interaction. | Synthesize information from multiple sources for a defined audience. | Shift register, manage nuance, and adapt politeness to institutional context. |
| C1.2 | Understand rhetorical structure, implied meaning, humor, irony, and varied speech rates. | Present, negotiate, and discuss with nuance, precision, and flexible register. | Read dense commentary, specialist-adjacent texts, literature excerpts, and research summaries. | Write sophisticated arguments, edited prose, and audience-sensitive summaries. | Handle conflict, ambiguity, and complex spontaneous exchange. | Condense, restructure, and adapt complex material across genres. | Use idiom, connotation, rhetorical conventions, and tone control. |
| C2.1 | Understand dense authentic speech, specialist discussion, literary/ironic language, and regional variation with strategy. | Communicate spontaneously with high precision across abstract, professional, and cultural topics. | Read specialist texts, literature, policy, essays, and complex commentary. | Write precise, stylistically controlled texts for different expert/non-expert audiences. | Lead nuanced interactions and adjust language in real time. | Reformulate abstract/specialist information accurately for different audiences. | Use sophisticated register, humor/irony awareness, and rhetorical framing. |
| C2.2 | Understand nearly all standard and varied authentic input, including implicit argument and stylistic effect. | Communicate with flexible style, rhetorical control, and high spontaneous accuracy. | Read, analyze, and critique complex literature, specialist texts, and dense argumentation. | Produce highly polished, precise, nuanced, and genre-appropriate writing. | Manage high-stakes discussion, negotiation, debate, and mediation. | Synthesize, critique, and reframe complex information with precision and audience fit. | Demonstrate mature sociolinguistic judgment, style, subtext, and cultural reference handling. |
